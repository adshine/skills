## When to use

- To choose exactly one option from two to about five.
- When comparing the options side by side helps people decide.

## When not to use

- **For more than about five options.** Use a [select](/components/select).
- **When people can choose several.** Use [checkboxes](/components/checkbox).
- **For a single on or off setting.** Use a [switch](/components/switch) or a single checkbox.

## Content

- The legend asks the question; the options answer it: "Billing cycle" then "Monthly", "Yearly".
- Keep options parallel in grammar and length, in sentence case, with no trailing punctuation.
- Order options logically: smallest to largest, most to least common, or a natural sequence.
- Preselect a safe default when there is one. Leave all unselected only when choosing is the point, like a survey answer.

## Keyboard

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Moves focus into the group, onto the selected option (or the first one) |
| <kbd>Arrow down</kbd> or <kbd>Arrow right</kbd> | Moves to and selects the next option |
| <kbd>Arrow up</kbd> or <kbd>Arrow left</kbd> | Moves to and selects the previous option |
| <kbd>Space</kbd> | Selects the focused option if it is not selected |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Leaves the group |

## Checklist

- The group has a name: wrap it in `fieldset` with a `legend`, or pass `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`.
- Each option has a `Label` linked with `htmlFor` and `id`.
- The group is one tab stop; arrow keys move within it. Radix handles this roving focus.
- The selected option is shown by a filled dot, not by color alone.
- Disabled options are skipped by arrow keys; explain nearby why they are unavailable.
- The clickable area (dot plus label) is at least 44 by 44 px on touch screens.
